How the community garden grew

By Ruth Okafor

Five years ago the corner of Maple Street was an empty lot with a broken fence. Today it feeds forty families, and every Saturday morning it fills with neighbours who had never spoken before the first seeds went in.

It started with Mrs Alvarez, who asked the council whether anyone minded if she planted tomatoes. Nobody did. By the end of that first summer there were twelve raised beds, a rain barrel donated by the hardware shop, and a rota pinned to the fence.

What grows there now

Beans, courgettes, three kinds of lettuce, and a stubborn fig tree that everyone said would never survive the winter. It did, and it is now the garden's unofficial mascot.

  • Twelve raised beds, wheelchair height
  • A seed library run by the children's reading group
  • A bench donated in memory of Mr Patel
"I can't kneel any more, so the high beds gave me my garden back," says Dorothy, 84.

How to join

Anyone living within a mile can have a bed. Ask at the library desk, or come along on a Saturday morning: there is always tea, and there is always something that needs watering.

The garden is looking for volunteers to help with the new accessible path, which will let more neighbours reach the beds without help. Building starts in the spring.
