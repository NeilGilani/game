# Outreach emails

*Drafts, 12 October 2026. Short on purpose: busy clinicians read the first three lines. Each asks for one small,
specific thing. Send from a real person's address, with a real signature. Never claim a user, result or
endorsement you don't have.*

**Before you send any of these**

- Attach or link the [one-page summary](one-page-summary.md), not the whole repository.
- Don't send a download link to a clinician or family as if AGENCY were ready for daily use. It isn't: nobody with
  ALS has used it yet, and the Mac app isn't signed. Offer a demo call instead.
- Keep a simple log of who you wrote to, when, and what they said (a spreadsheet is fine).
- `[[TEAM: choose a shared contact address for AGENCY (the site's CONTACT_EMAIL is still empty) and a signature
  block: name, "co-founder, AGENCY", "student, UC Santa Cruz", phone if you want calls.]]`

## 1. ALS clinic speech-language pathologists

**Who:** SLPs at ALS clinics and multidisciplinary ALS centres (for example the Forbes Norris MDA/ALS Research and
Treatment Center at CPMC, the UCSF ALS Center, and ALS Network-affiliated clinics in California). Find them through
the clinic's team page or ALS Network's care services staff.
`[[TEAM: names and addresses; ask ALS Network for a warm introduction rather than cold-emailing clinic staff.]]`

> **Subject:** 30 minutes of your AAC expertise for a free eye-gaze project?
>
> Dear `[[TEAM: name]]`,
>
> We are three UC Santa Cruz students building AGENCY, a free, open-source program that lets someone who can only
> move their eyes use a Mac with the built-in webcam: an eye keyboard and phrase board that speak aloud, AI
> suggestions to speed up typing, everyday tasks that only happen after the person approves them, and a way to
> call family by blinking.
>
> Nobody with ALS has used it yet, and we don't want them to until people like you have told us what we're getting
> wrong. Would you give us 30 minutes, by video or in person, to watch a demo and tell us:
>
> - what would stop you recommending it to a client;
> - what you'd want measured before you trusted it; and
> - whether you'd consider advising us (paid, at your usual rate if your employer allows).
>
> It isn't a medical device and we're careful not to describe it as one. A one-page summary is attached.
>
> Thank you for considering it,
> `[[TEAM: signature]]`

## 2. ALS Network's Augmentative Communication Equipment Loan Program

**Who:** the team that runs ALS Network's free AAC equipment loans (California and Hawaii). The program page lists
eye trackers, head trackers, switches and speech-generating devices "as available", and says to contact a care
manager or call 818-865-8067; general care services are careservices@alsnetwork.org, 1-866-750-2572
([program page](https://alsnetwork.org/our-care-services/augmentative-communication-equipment-loan-program/), checked
12 October 2026). The program serves people with ALS, so write to it as a professional asking for advice, not as a
borrower. `[[TEAM: ask whether there's a staff email for AAC/AT professionals rather than the client line.]]`

> **Subject:** A free eye-control program, and a request for your advice
>
> Hello,
>
> We're building AGENCY, a free, open-source program that turns an ordinary Mac and its webcam into an eye-controlled
> computer: an eye keyboard and phrase board that speak aloud, help with everyday tasks after the person approves
> each one, and a blink signal that keeps calling family until someone answers. We're three UC Santa Cruz students,
> and we're setting AGENCY up as a nonprofit.
>
> Your program sees what people with ALS actually need from communication equipment, and when. We'd be grateful
> for a short call with someone on your AAC team to ask:
>
> 1. where a free webcam option could help the people you serve (for example while they wait for a loaned device,
>    or alongside one), and where it would get in the way;
> 2. whether we could, later, borrow an infrared eye tracker for a few weeks to measure AGENCY honestly against it;
>    and
> 3. whether anyone with ALS or a caregiver you work with might like to advise us (paid).
>
> AGENCY isn't a medical device or an emergency service, and nobody with ALS has used it yet. We want your advice
> before that happens, not after.
>
> With thanks,
> `[[TEAM: signature]]`

## 3. Ability Tools (California's Assistive Technology Act program)

**Who:** Ability Tools, California's AT Act program, run by the California Foundation for Independent Living Centers
(CFILC). Since a 2026 procurement, lending goes through three regional Device Lending and Demonstration Centers
(Northern: Community Resources for Independent Living; Central: Central Coast Assistive Technology Center; Southern:
Center for Applied Rehabilitation Technology), with loans of up to 30 days
([DLDC page](https://abilitytools.org/device-lending-and-demonstration-centers-dldc/)). The site's "find a DLDC"
links said "Coming Soon" on 12 October 2026. Contact listed in the national AT Act data report:
info@abilitytools.org, 916-390-2690 ([report](https://catada.info/apr_reports25/california_2025)).
`[[TEAM: we couldn't confirm that any DLDC lends eye-gaze devices; ask. UCSC is in the Central region.]]`

> **Subject:** Borrowing an eye-gaze device to test a free, open-source alternative
>
> Hello,
>
> We're three UC Santa Cruz students building AGENCY, a free, open-source program that lets people who can only move
> their eyes use a Mac with its built-in webcam.
>
> Two requests:
>
> 1. **Borrowing.** We'd like to borrow an infrared eye tracker (or an eye-gaze communication device) to measure
>    how far behind a webcam AGENCY really is. We'd publish the numbers, good or bad. Are we eligible to borrow,
>    and what's the process?
> 2. **Later, a listing.** Once AGENCY has been tested with real users, would Ability Tools consider showing it at
>    demonstrations as a free option alongside the devices you lend? We're not asking for that now.
>
> Thank you,
> `[[TEAM: signature]]`
