### Scientific Abstract

Most people with ALS lose the ability to meet their daily communication needs through speech (Beukelman et al.,
2011). Eye-gaze systems restore communication, but everyday eye typing is slow, about 8 words per minute in a
long-term field study (Cai et al., 2023), and most systems help people talk but not act: sending a message, booking
an appointment or ordering food still needs a helper. AGENCY is free, open-source software that turns an ordinary
Mac and its built-in webcam into an eye-controlled computer. It combines dwell-based gaze selection, switch scanning
and a head pointer; a talk board and eye keyboard with optional AI suggestions; an AI agent that carries out
everyday digital tasks but stops for the person's explicit approval before anything consequential; and a help
signal (three long blinks) that keeps alerting chosen family members until one confirms they are coming. A working
prototype exists and is covered by automated tests, but it has not yet been used by anyone with ALS. In this
project we will (1) make AGENCY safe and reliable enough for supervised use, including support for infrared
trackers that people already own, (2) run an IRB-approved feasibility study with people with ALS and their
caregivers, co-designed with paid advisors with lived experience and AAC clinicians, and (3) release a signed,
documented version free of charge through partners such as ALS clinics and equipment loan programs.

### Scientific Rationale and Potential Impact

**The need.** Between 80 and 95% of people with ALS become unable to meet daily communication needs with natural
speech (Beukelman, Fager & Nordness, 2011). Eye gaze is often the last reliable movement, but eye-gaze typing has
been measured at 8.1 ± 3.9 words per minute in daily use (Cai et al., 2023), against roughly 180 for speech (Kane
et al., 2017). People with ALS also tell researchers that being themselves, with humour and timing, matters as much
as speed (Kane et al., 2017). Large language models can help: in a small study, an LLM abbreviation interface raised
text-entry rates by 29 to 60% for two eye-gaze users with ALS (Cai et al., 2024). Caregivers carry a heavy load,
with a median of about three hours of care a day in a German multicentre study, and more for people who use
ventilation (Schischlevskij et al., 2021).

**The gap.** Built-in tools now offer free camera-based eye control (Apple Eye Tracking on iPhone and iPad; Windows
Eye Control with supported infrared trackers; Google's Look to Speak). They move a pointer or pick phrases. They
don't help someone complete a multi-step task safely, and they don't help them summon a person when they can't
speak. Dedicated eye-gaze communication devices are effective but costly and often arrive late in the disease.
`[[TEAM: add a sourced figure for device cost and wait times if an SLP advisor can give you one; otherwise leave this
general.]]`

**What AGENCY adds.** One free program, on hardware many families already own, that lets the person (a) talk, with
AI help that runs only when they choose it; (b) get things done through an agent that plans the steps, shows exactly
what will happen, and acts only after the person approves, with a Stop that always works; and (c) call for help by
blinking, with alerts that keep going until a person answers. Webcam tracking is the free entry point, not the
ceiling: the project adds support for infrared trackers for people who need more precision.

**Impact if successful.** A free, open, documented option that ALS clinics and equipment loan programs can offer on
the first visit, including while someone waits for a funded device or alongside one; evidence, published openly,
on who it works for and who it doesn't; and open code other AAC projects can reuse.

### Technology Summary

AGENCY runs on macOS as a desktop app and in a browser as a web demo. Its parts, all in one public repository
(github.com/NeilGilani/game):

- **Input.** Webcam gaze estimation using Google's MediaPipe face landmarks and an open-source eye-image model, run
  on the computer; camera frames are never stored or sent. A nine-point calibration reports its accuracy on targets
  it didn't train on, and calls poor calibration poor. Five input modes drive every screen: gaze with dwell, head
  pointer with dwell, scanning with a long-blink switch, scanning with a key switch, and mouse or touch for a helper.
  If tracking is lost, it switches to scanning automatically.
- **Talk.** Phrase boards and an eye keyboard that speak aloud through the computer's voices, with optional AI
  sentence suggestions.
- **Agent.** Tasks are planned as typed steps, through an allow-list of tools. Every consequential step (sending,
  buying, signing, submitting) needs an approval tied to a fingerprint of the exact action; the confirming target
  appears elsewhere on screen so a lingering look can't confirm by accident. Results are checked against the
  service's own records before AGENCY says something happened. The agent never handles passwords, two-factor codes
  or card numbers.
- **Help signal.** Three long blinks (or looking at Help) start an alert that contacts chosen people in order, then
  everyone, repeatedly, until someone confirms "I'm on my way". It is designed as a caregiver-alerting tool, not a
  medical alarm or emergency service, and is not connected to any medical equipment.

**Stage today: working prototype (Stage 2).** The software won 1st place in the Cloud AI track at the UCSC AI
Hackathon. Automated tests cover the workflow engine, approvals, the help-signal escalation and the gaze pipeline.
What isn't done yet: nobody with ALS has used AGENCY; all gaze-accuracy figures so far come from simulated faces;
the Mac app isn't yet signed or notarized; real-world services and messages are simulated by default. This project
closes those gaps.

### Specific Aims

**Aim 1. Make AGENCY ready for supervised use by people with ALS (months 1 to 9).** Sign and notarize the Mac app;
add support for infrared eye trackers alongside the webcam; add fatigue-aware settings (longer dwell on request, a
"rest my eyes" mode that keeps the help signal active) and on-device word prediction; make cloud AI optional and
off-by-default for study use, on paid terms that don't allow training on users' content; add opt-in, numbers-only
study logging. *Milestone:* a release passing a written safety checklist reviewed by the advisory group.
*Go/no-go:* team and volunteer testing shows selection accuracy and help-signal reliability good enough to put in
front of participants, against thresholds the advisors set in advance.

**Aim 2. Co-design and test with people with ALS and caregivers (months 4 to 18).** Form a paid advisory group of
people with ALS, caregivers and AAC clinicians. Run an IRB-approved feasibility study `[[TEAM: number, set with
advisors and a statistician; the draft protocol suggests 12 to 15]]` of people with ALS who use or are candidates for
eye-gaze AAC, at home or in clinic, with existing communication aids and alarms left in place. Measures: selection
accuracy and accidental selections; communication rate with and without AI suggestions; task completion and
approval errors; usability (System Usability Scale), device satisfaction (QUEST 2.0) and psychosocial impact
(PIADS); fatigue; help-signal success and false-trigger rates in drills; caregiver time and burden. *Milestone:*
published results, including what didn't work.

**Aim 3. Make it available free, with the people who already support families (months 12 to 24).** Release a
signed, documented version; write setup and training guides for SLPs, OTs and families; work with
`[[TEAM: partners who have agreed, e.g. ALS Network's equipment loan program]]` to offer AGENCY at assessment and
loan visits; publish everything openly. *Milestone:* AGENCY offered through at least `[[TEAM: number]]` clinics or
loan programs, with a support process in place.

### Commercialization Plan

*Rename the heading only if ProposalCentral allows. Otherwise keep "Commercialization Plan" and open with the
first sentence below.*

AGENCY will be distributed **free of charge** and its source published under an open-source licence
(`[[TEAM: AGPL-3.0-or-later, if chosen]]`), so families never pay for it and no one can take it closed. "Bringing it
to market" for AGENCY means getting it into the hands of people with ALS through the channels they already use:

- **Distribution.** Signed downloads from AGENCY's website; offered at assessment by ALS clinic SLPs; through
  equipment loan programs and California's AT Act lending centres; listed alongside other free AAC tools. It runs on
  Macs many families already own, with no extra hardware for the webcam option.
- **Sustainability.** `[[TEAM: applicant organisation]]` is a nonprofit funded by grants and donations, never by
  users. The plan covers a small team after the award through a mix of foundation grants (several California and
  national disability-technology funders are in our pipeline), donations, and in-kind support (developer program
  fee waivers, nonprofit cloud credits). Running costs are kept low by design: computation runs on the user's
  computer, and cloud AI is optional.
- **Revenue.** We don't expect revenue from users. If the funded work ever generates revenue, we accept the
  Association's revenue-sharing terms.
- **IP.** `[[TEAM: applicant]]` holds the copyright in AGENCY's code through written assignments from each author.
  Third-party components are open source (Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD, OFL, AGPL); none needs a paid licence for this use.
  `[[TEAM: confirm the last point once RealEye has answered; see licence-decision-memo.md.]]`
- **Regulatory path.** AGENCY is described and distributed as general-purpose computer-access and communication
  software, not a medical device, and makes no diagnostic or treatment claims. We will obtain regulatory advice
  before the release in Aim 3, because FDA classifies "powered communication systems" (21 CFR 890.3710) and "powered
  environmental control systems" (21 CFR 890.3725) as Class II devices exempt from 510(k). If advice is that a
  feature makes AGENCY a device, we'll either change the feature or follow that path deliberately.
- **Timeline.** A free public release at the end of the award (month 24), which is within the Association's
  3-to-5-year expectation.
